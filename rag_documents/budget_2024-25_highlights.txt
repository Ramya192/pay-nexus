Budget 2024-25 — Income Tax Highlights for Salaried Employees

Compiled from public secondary sources (newsonair.gov.in press releases, business-standard.com, and
others), cross-checked across multiple sources — NOT the verbatim Finance Bill text. Presented
23 July 2024, effective FY 2024-25 (AY 2025-26) unless noted. Superseded on some figures by Budget
2025-26 (see budget_2025-26_highlights.txt) — where the two conflict, the 2025-26 document's figures
are more current.

New tax regime slabs (FY 2024-25, before Budget 2025-26's further revision):
Up to Rs 3,00,000: Nil
Rs 3,00,001 to Rs 7,00,000: 5 percent
Rs 7,00,001 to Rs 10,00,000: 10 percent
Rs 10,00,001 to Rs 12,00,000: 15 percent
Rs 12,00,001 to Rs 15,00,000: 20 percent
Above Rs 15,00,000: 30 percent

Standard deduction (new regime): increased from Rs 50,000 to Rs 75,000 — this is the change that
introduced the current split between the new regime (Rs 75,000) and the unchanged old regime
(Rs 50,000). Estimated saving of up to Rs 17,500 for a salaried employee under the new regime as a
direct result of this one change.

Family pension deduction: increased from Rs 15,000 to Rs 25,000.

Employer NPS contribution deduction (Section 80CCD(2)): the deductible share of an employer's NPS
contribution, for employees under the new regime, was raised from 10% to 14% of salary (basic + DA).
The old regime's 10% limit was not changed.

TDS on rent (Section 194-IB): the TDS rate on rent payments was reduced to 2% (from 5%), effective
1 October 2024. (Separately, the ANNUAL THRESHOLD at which TDS on rent applies at all was later
raised from Rs 2.40 lakh to Rs 6 lakh in Budget 2025-26 — a different change to a different
parameter of the same provision; see budget_2025-26_highlights.txt.)

Employer PF contribution scheme: a new government-funded direct PF contribution for eligible
employees earning up to Rs 1 lakh/month, aimed at incentivizing formal-sector hiring for an
estimated 210 lakh (21 million) young employees.
