# Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026 — Payslip Format & the 50% Wage Rule

## What was notified, and when

The four Labour Codes (Code on Wages, Industrial Relations Code, Code on Social Security, Occupational
Safety Health & Working Conditions Code) became legally effective **21 November 2025**. The Ministry of
Labour & Employment then notified the **Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026** — the operational rules
implementing the Code on Wages specifically — on **8 May 2026**, following public consultation on a
2025 draft. These Central Rules consolidate 17 legacy wage-related rule sets into one document covering
minimum wages, deductions, working hours, weekly rest, and wage-slip issuance for central-sphere
establishments.

## Form V — the standardized payslip format

The Central Rules prescribe **Form V** as the mandatory wage-slip (payslip) format: every employer must
issue a wage slip to each employee, in this format, on or before the payment of wages. A Form V wage
slip must include, at minimum: the establishment's name and address; the date of issue; the wage period
it covers; the employee's name, Father's/Mother's/Spouse's name, and designation; Universal Account
Number (UAN); and bank account number — alongside the actual earnings/deductions breakdown. This is the
first time a single, uniform payslip layout has been centrally mandated rather than left to each
employer's own format.

## The 50% wage rule

The Code on Wages' own definition of "wages" caps how much of a employee's total remuneration can sit
in exempted/excluded components (HRA, conveyance, bonus, overtime, and similar allowances) — **these
exclusions cannot exceed 50% of total CTC**. Equivalently: **Basic Pay + Dearness Allowance + Retaining
Allowance must together be at least 50% of total CTC.** If a company's actual salary structure exceeds
the 50% exclusion cap, the excess is added back into "wages" for the purpose of statutory calculations
(PF, gratuity, etc.) regardless of what the payslip itself labels it as.

## Why this changes what a payslip looks like

Before this rule, many Indian salary structures kept Basic Pay deliberately low (often 30-35% of CTC)
specifically to minimize PF/gratuity contributions, which are calculated on Basic + DA. Restructuring to
meet the 50% floor means:
- **Basic Pay rises as a share of CTC** (commonly from ~30-35% up to the 50% floor) — visibly more of
  the payslip's earnings side now sits under "Basic" than before.
- **PF and gratuity contributions rise** correspondingly, since both are computed on Basic + DA.
- **Take-home pay can drop at an unchanged CTC**, purely from this restructuring — many employers offset
  this with a small compensating gross-up to CTC rather than passing the full reduction through, but
  that's a company-by-company choice, not something the Rules themselves require.

This directly matters for a "why did my take-home drop" question: a real, valid answer for 2026 can
be "your salary structure was restructured to comply with the Code on Wages' 50% rule," not just a
month-to-month TDS/bonus fluctuation.

## Notes

- This is central-government-rules coverage — individual states can (and are expected to) notify their
  own corresponding rules under the same Codes; a state government employee's exact timeline may differ
  from the central notification date above.
- "Wages" under this Code has its own statutory definition distinct from "salary" as used elsewhere in
  this app's other documents (e.g. `it_act_key_sections.md`'s income-tax definitions) — the 50% rule is
  a labour-law/PF-contribution concept, not an income-tax one; it doesn't itself change how income tax
  or TDS is computed.
