# Form 16 — Part A and Part B Structure

**Compiled from public secondary sources (taggd.in, paisabazaar.com, 1finance.co.in, and others),
cross-checked across multiple sources — NOT the verbatim official form specification.**

## What it is

The certificate an employer issues to a salaried employee confirming tax was deducted at source
(TDS) from their salary and deposited with the Income Tax Department during the financial year.
Only issued to employees who actually had TDS deducted.

## Part A — TDS summary, generated via TRACES

Generated and digitally signed through the government's TRACES portal, not just typed up by the
employer — this is what makes it an authoritative TDS record, not merely a payslip summary.

Contains:
- Employer's name, address, TAN, and PAN
- Employee's name, address, and PAN
- Period of employment with this employer during the financial year
- Quarter-by-quarter summary of TDS deducted and deposited
- Challan Identification Numbers (CIN) and TDS receipt numbers for each deposit

## Part B — Salary breakdown and computed tax liability

An annexure to Part A, prepared and certified by the employer (not machine-generated via TRACES the
way Part A is) — details how the employer arrived at the final TDS figure.

Contains:
- Gross salary breakdown: Basic, HRA, Special Allowance, bonus/incentives, and other components
- Exemptions claimed under Section 10 — HRA exemption (10(13A)), LTA, gratuity exemption, etc.
- Deductions claimed under Chapter VI-A — 80C, 80D, and others, if the employee declared them and
  submitted proof to the employer
- The standard deduction (Section 16(ia))
- Net taxable salary and the resulting computed tax liability for the year

## Why both parts matter for reconciliation

Part A confirms TDS was *actually deposited* with the government (traceable via CIN/challan
numbers) — Part B explains *how* the employer arrived at that TDS figure from the salary structure
and declared deductions. A mismatch between Part B's declared deductions and what was actually
filed in the employee's own ITR is a common source of tax-notice issues — Part B is the employer's
computation, not a guarantee the employee's own return will match it exactly if their actual
declared investments differed from what they told the employer during the year.
